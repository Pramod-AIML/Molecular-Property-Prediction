Q1: Why does Extra Trees (R² = 0.9029) outperform Deep Neural Networks on this dataset?

Answer: Deep neural networks struggle on the Delaney dataset (1,128 samples) with 2,068 sparse binary features because the sample-to-feature ratio (n / p ≈ 0.55) causes severe overparameterization. Extremely Randomized Trees (Extra Trees) draw random linear cut-points across subsets of features, suppressing variance on bit collision noise and preventing overfitting.

Q2: What is the scientific basis of the Local Explainability (XAI) equation?

Answer: The weight formula w_i = -1.2 · LogP_i + 0.8 · |q_i| fuses Wildman-Crippen atomic partition contributions with Gasteiger partial electrostatic charges. Atoms with negative LogP and high partial charge increase hydration enthalpy with solvent water molecules, driving dissolution.

Q3: Why are PAINS alerts critical in virtual screening?

Answer: Pan-Assay Interference Compounds (PAINS) contain promiscuous chemical motifs (e.g. rhodanines, quinones) that produce false-positive readouts by covalent protein modification, metal chelation, or light scatter. Automated filtering saves millions in wet-lab assay costs.
